Beginner plant guide

Ten forgiving houseplants for Dutch homes.

“Easy” means the plant’s needs overlap with your room and routine, not that the plant can live anywhere.

Short answer

Reliable options include Pothos, heartleaf Philodendron, ZZ plant, snake plant, Aspidistra, spider plant, Pilea, rubber plant, Monstera and Hoya. Match the species to your actual window light, use a draining pot, and water from soil checks rather than a fixed schedule.

Choose from your constraints

If you forget to water

ZZ plant, snake plant and many Hoyas store water and usually cope better with a missed watering than with repeated wet soil. “Drought tolerant” still does not mean abandoning the plant for months.

If your window is north-facing

Try Aspidistra, Pothos, heartleaf Philodendron, ZZ plant or snake plant close to the window. A north-facing sill may still be much brighter than a table three metres away. See the lower-light plant guide.

If you want visible growth

Pothos, heartleaf Philodendron, spider plant and Pilea can be rewarding in sufficient light. Their faster feedback also makes watering or light problems easier to notice.

If you want one large plant

A rubber plant or supported Monstera can become architectural. Confirm mature width, ceiling height, pot weight and access before buying a large specimen.

The Dutch-winter filter

KNMI’s climate information makes clear that sunless winter days are part of life here. A plant that feels easy in June can become difficult if it remains far from the window while watering stays unchanged. Move plants closer to light, protect them from cold glass and radiator blasts, and allow longer drying intervals.

Three buying rules

  1. Buy for the position. Decide the window and distance first.
  2. Inspect before paying. Look under leaves and around new growth for pests.
  3. Start smaller. A manageable plant lets you learn its rhythm at lower cost.
Pet household? “Easy care” does not mean pet-safe. Check the exact botanical name against a trusted veterinary toxicity database before bringing it home, and place plants according to your animal’s behaviour.
