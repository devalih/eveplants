Where to buy houseplants in the Netherlands.

The “best” shop depends on whether you want rare plants, organic growing, rescued plants, in-person advice or a broad local selection.

By Eve Plants24 August 20268 min read

Short answer

For specialist online choice, start with PLNTS. For rescued-value boxes, try Plantje.nl. For organically grown kamerplanten, look at Sprinklr. In Amsterdam, Wildernis offers an independent shop experience. For a broad in-person range around the country, Intratuin is practical. A good local independent nursery may still be your best first stop.

This is an independent editorial shortlist, not a paid ranking. Stock, delivery areas and policies change, so check the shop before travelling or ordering.

Five useful places to start

1. PLNTS — specialist online range

PLNTS is useful when you want to compare many indoor species, sizes and care levels in one place. The catalogue is especially convenient for collectors. Online plants experience transport stress, so unpack promptly, inspect carefully and give the plant time to settle before repotting.

2. Plantje.nl — convenient bundles and “kneusjes”

Plantje.nl sells individual plants and boxes shipped directly to homes. Its “kneusjes” concept gives cosmetically imperfect or surplus plants another chance. It can be good value, but a rescue box is best for someone comfortable identifying different care needs.

3. Sprinklr — organically grown options

Sprinklr maintains a collection of biologically grown houseplants. This is a useful starting point if growing method matters to you. “Organic” does not remove the need to inspect for pests or match the species to your room.

4. Wildernis — independent Amsterdam shop

Wildernis combines plants, pots and an in-person Amsterdam setting. A physical specialist is valuable when you want to see the exact plant, ask questions and compare how different specimens have grown.

5. Intratuin — broad, accessible in-person choice

Intratuin has garden centres across the Netherlands and a broad kamerplanten range. Stock and expertise vary by branch, but the ability to inspect the actual plant and find pots or substrate on the same trip is practical.

Also search locally. Try “kamerplantenwinkel”, “kwekerij” or “tuincentrum” plus your city. Independent growers and neighbourhood shops can offer fresher stock, local knowledge and less transport.

Choose the room before the plant

The healthiest specimen will struggle in the wrong position. Before buying, note the direction and distance of the nearest window, whether direct sun reaches the spot, radiator proximity, available width and height, and how often you realistically check soil. Bring a phone photo of the room to the shop.

A two-minute healthy-plant check

  • Inspect leaf undersides, new growth and stem joints for webbing, scale, white fluff or moving insects.
  • Look for firm growth and a reasonable amount of clean new foliage. One damaged old leaf is not automatically a problem.
  • Smell the soil. Persistently sour, saturated compost can indicate poor root conditions.
  • Check whether roots are severely circling or pushing the plant out of the pot. Some roots at drainage holes are normal.
  • Ask when it was watered and how long it has been in the shop.
  • Keep a new arrival apart from the rest of your collection for a short observation period.

Online or in person?

Buy in person when you want to choose one exact specimen, compare condition or get room-specific advice. Online ordering is useful for range and convenience, but weather and transport add risk. Avoid having delicate tropical plants sit in a parcel network during heat, frost or when nobody can receive them.
