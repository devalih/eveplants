Good watering
practices.

Water the root ball properly, use water at a gentle temperature, and let the plant, pot and season decide the rhythm. Dutch tap water is usually a perfectly good place to start.

Short answer

For most houseplants, water slowly and evenly until the whole root ball is moist and a little water runs through the drainage holes. Empty the outer pot or saucer afterwards, then wait until the mix has dried to the level that suits that plant. Room-temperature tap water is ideal. In Amsterdam and much of the Netherlands, tap water is suitable for most plants, so solve light, drainage and watering rhythm before blaming the water.

How much water does a plant need?

The useful amount is not a fixed number of millilitres. It changes with the plant’s root mass, pot size, mix, light, season, temperature and humidity. The aim is simple: moisten the whole root ball, not just the top centimetre of compost.

Water slowly over the surface until a small amount drains from the bottom. This tells you the mix has been wetted more evenly. Let it drain for a few minutes, then empty the cachepot, tray or saucer. Roots need air as well as water, so a pot should never sit in runoff for days.

Plant or conditionWhat to check before watering again
Most leafy tropical plantsLet the upper few centimetres of mix dry, while deeper mix remains lightly moist.
Succulents, snake plants and ZZ plantsLet much more of the mix dry before watering thoroughly again.
Ferns and moisture-loving plantsDo not let the root ball become bone-dry, but still avoid a heavy, stagnant pot.
Winter or a low-light roomExpect slower drying. Check the mix rather than following a summer schedule.

Frequent tiny sips are a common problem: the surface looks cared for while the middle of the pot stays dry or, in a dense mix, stays wet for too long. Feel the soil with a finger, lift the pot to learn its dry and freshly watered weight, or use a wooden skewer for deeper pots.

What temperature should the water be?

Use water at room temperature, or at least close to the room’s temperature. This is especially helpful for tropical houseplants and plants in a cool winter room. Very cold water straight from a cold tap can be an unnecessary shock to warm roots; very hot water is never appropriate.

There is no need to heat the water precisely. A watering can that has come to room temperature is enough. If your tap runs very cold in winter, fill the can first and let it stand indoors while you check the plants.

Should water be settled, filtered or straight from the tap?

For ordinary houseplant care, freshly drawn Dutch tap water is usually fine. Letting water stand can bring it to room temperature, but it does not meaningfully remove the calcium and magnesium that create hardness. It is therefore not a substitute for a lower-mineral water source when mineral buildup is genuinely the issue.

Amsterdam water is soft, at about 7.8 °dH, according to Waternet. Water hardness is mainly calcium and magnesium, and it varies by supplier and location. Vitens notes that most Dutch drinking water is centrally softened where needed, although your postcode can differ. Dutch drinking water is tightly regulated, and Waternet says chlorine and other treatment chemicals are used as little as possible.

For most plants, this means tap water is a sensible default. Consider a measured trial of rainwater, filtered water or a lower-mineral source only for a plant that repeatedly shows mineral buildup or sensitivity after you have checked roots, pot drainage, light, fertiliser and dry air. Use collected rainwater only when it is clean and stored hygienically.

When water quality may matter

A white crust on the pot or soil, recurring tip damage on a sensitive plant, or a very small pot that is watered and fertilised often can justify looking closer. But old brown edges will not turn green when the water changes. Judge the next leaves, root health and the plant’s overall growth.

One change at a time. Do not change the water, pot, soil, fertiliser and position together. Stabilise the basics first, then test one adjustment for several weeks.

A reliable watering routine

  1. Check the soil lower down, not just its surface.
  2. Water at room temperature when the plant has reached its appropriate dry point.
  3. Water slowly until a little drains out.
  4. Let the pot drain, then empty the saucer or outer pot.
  5. Adjust for light and season, especially during a Dutch winter.
