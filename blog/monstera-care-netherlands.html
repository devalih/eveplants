Plant guide · Monstera deliciosa

Monstera care in a Dutch home.

Give it useful light, an airy root zone and something to climb; let the plant’s growth tell you when conditions are working.

Short answer

Place Monstera deliciosa in bright indirect light, water thoroughly after the upper potting mix has partly dried, use a draining chunky mix and give mature stems a support. In Dutch winter, move it closer to light and expect slower drying.

Care at a glance

LightBright, filtered light; gentle direct sun may be useful after gradual acclimation
WaterThoroughly, then allow the upper layer to dry; never leave the nursery pot standing in water
SoilAiry, moisture-retentive but free-draining mix in a pot with holes
GrowthClimber; support the main stem and aerial roots
FeedingModerate feed during active growth; reduce when growth slows
SafetyContains calcium oxalate and can irritate if chewed; keep away from children and pets

Light and watering

Monstera can survive in medium light, but larger leaves and natural splits are more likely with stronger usable light and maturity. Put it near an east, south or west window with the intensity moderated as needed. In October, reassess plants that spent summer several metres into the room.

Check moisture below the surface. When the upper portion has dried, soak the root ball evenly and drain completely. The interval may be short in warm summer growth and much longer in a grey Dutch December. Calendar watering is the common mistake.

Soil and repotting

Use a mix that holds some moisture but contains coarse structure for air. A slightly snug pot is easier to manage than an oversized one. Repot when roots substantially fill the pot, water runs through channels, or the mix has collapsed, not just because an aerial root appears.

Help it behave like a climber

Monstera grows up trees in nature. Identify the back of the stem, where aerial roots emerge, and place a firm plank, coir pole or moss pole there. Tie the stem loosely; do not bind leaf stalks. Face the leaves toward the light and avoid rotating constantly. A supported plant often becomes more coherent and may develop larger leaves.

Read the leaves

SymptomLikely checks
Yellow leaves plus wet mixDrainage, low light, pot size and root condition
Brown dry edgesUneven watering, root stress, hot radiator air or accumulated salts
No splitsPlant age, light and climbing support
Long small growthInsufficient light
Black cold patchesCold glass, draught or chilling during transport
New plant? Let it acclimate before repotting unless the root system is in immediate danger. Shipping, a new room and a new pot all at once create unnecessary stress.
