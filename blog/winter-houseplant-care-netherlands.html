Dutch seasonal care

Winter houseplant care in the Netherlands.

In winter, the goal is usually not vigorous growth. It is stable roots, enough light and fewer well-timed interventions.

By Eve Plants24 August 20268 min read

Short answer

Move plants closer to suitable windows, check soil instead of following a calendar, water less often when growth slows, pause routine feeding, and protect foliage from both cold glass and hot radiator air. Keep watching for pests and root stress.

Dutch winter changes several variables at once. Days shorten, overcast stretches reduce usable light, windows become cold, and central heating dries the air. KNMI climate information notes that fully sunless days are normal in winter. A care routine that worked in June can therefore become too wet or too dark by December.

Give plants more of the light that exists

Move light-hungry tropical plants closer to a window, but keep tender leaves from resting against cold glass. Clean dusty leaves and windows so available light is not unnecessarily blocked. A south- or west-facing position that was too intense in summer may be useful in winter; still watch for sudden bright spells.

Rotate only when uneven growth bothers you, and do it gradually. Constant turning makes a climbing plant repeatedly reorient its leaves.

Stop watering by weekday

Lower light usually means slower photosynthesis and slower water use. Check several centimetres into the potting mix, feel the pot’s weight, and look at the plant before watering. Then water thoroughly and let excess drain. Small ceremonial sips can leave part of the root ball dry while encouraging shallow roots.

Avoid the radiator–window trap

A windowsill can be bright but thermally extreme: cold at night, direct radiator heat below, and a warm room by day. Move sensitive plants slightly inward or sideways while preserving light. Keep leaves away from draughts at frequently opened doors and windows.

Grouping plants can modestly buffer conditions, but it does not create rainforest humidity. If a humidity-loving plant repeatedly crisps, measure the room with a hygrometer before buying a humidifier. Pebble trays have limited effect on room humidity.

Feeding, repotting and cleaning

When a plant is not actively growing, routine fertiliser can accumulate in the mix without being used. Pause or reduce feeding according to the species and actual growth. Avoid unnecessary repotting in the darkest months; act sooner only for rot, a broken pot or another urgent root problem. Keep leaves clean and remove dead material, but avoid heavy pruning unless needed.

A five-minute winter check

  1. Look at both sides of new leaves for pests.
  2. Feel the potting mix at depth and lift the pot.
  3. Check that the pot is not standing in drained water.
  4. Notice cold glass, draughts and radiator airflow.
  5. Compare the plant with a photo from two weeks earlier.
Winter rule: change one thing at a time. Moving, repotting, fertilising and treating a plant simultaneously makes it hard to learn what helped, and adds stress.
