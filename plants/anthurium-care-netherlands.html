Plant guide · Anthurium andraeanum

Flamingo flower care in the Netherlands.

A practical profile for Amsterdam apartments and Dutch homes, from summer window light to winter heating.

Short answer

Keep Anthurium andraeanum in bright filtered light, an airy epiphytic mix and steady warmth. Water after the upper mix begins to dry, drain fully, and protect leaves from radiator air.

Care at a glance

Botanical nameAnthurium andraeanum
LightBright indirect light; avoid abrupt midday sun
WaterWater after slight upper drying; never leave saturated
SoilChunky, organic and airy epiphytic-style mix
TemperatureWarm with moderate-to-higher humidity
FeedingDilute feed during active leaves and flowers

Does it fit an Amsterdam home?

Anthurium can perform well near a bright east or west window. In deep rooms, colourful spathes fade and new growth becomes weaker.

Window direction is only the beginning. Opposite buildings, balconies, trees, privacy film and distance from the glass all change the usable light. Check the intended position at midday before buying, and remember that a spot that works in June may not work in December.

Water the root ball, not the calendar

Water after slight upper drying; never leave saturated Use a nursery pot or another container with drainage holes, water the root ball evenly, and empty the cachepot afterwards. Pot size, root mass, light and room temperature determine the interval; the day of the week does not.

Chunky, organic and airy epiphytic-style mix Repot when roots and mix indicate a need rather than immediately after purchase. A much larger pot stays wet longer and can turn a simple watering error into root damage.

What changes in Dutch winter?

Keep it away from a radiator and cold night-time glass. Lower winter growth requires less frequent water and little feeding.

During long grey stretches, clean dusty leaves, use the brightest suitable position and pause routine feeding when the plant is not growing. Keep foliage clear of both hot radiator air and cold glass. Make one change at a time so you can see what helped.

Read the plant’s signals

What you seeWhat to check first
Brown marginsDry air, salts or root stress
No new flowersLow light, maturity or nutrition
Yellow leavesWet roots or ageing

One imperfect old leaf rarely requires an emergency response. Look for a pattern across new growth, soil moisture and roots, and compare with a dated photo before changing several variables.

How to propagate it

Divide established rooted growths during repotting. Stem sections with nodes can be rooted in warm humid conditions.

Use clean tools, label the cutting with its date and keep new propagation in stable bright indirect light while roots establish. Share healthy, pest-free material through a local stekjesbieb or plant swap.

Safety at home

Anthurium contains calcium oxalate crystals and can irritate the mouth and skin. Keep away from children and pets.
