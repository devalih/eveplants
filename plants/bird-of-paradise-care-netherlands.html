Bird of paradise care in the Netherlands.

A practical profile for Amsterdam apartments and Dutch homes, from summer window light to winter heating.

Short answer

Give bird of paradise the brightest warm position available, including acclimated direct sun, water after the upper mix partly dries, and allow generous space for the leaves. Indoor flowering requires maturity and exceptional light.

Care at a glance

Botanical nameStrelitzia reginae / Strelitzia nicolai
LightVery bright light with acclimated direct sun
WaterRegular in active growth after upper drying; less in winter
SoilRich, structured and free-draining mix
TemperatureWarm; protect from cold glass and draughts
FeedingRegular balanced feed during strong growth

Does it fit an Amsterdam home?

Large Strelitzia is often sold as instant interior architecture. Measure the window, ceiling, route upstairs and mature spread before bringing one into a compact flat.

Window direction is only the beginning. Opposite buildings, balconies, trees, privacy film and distance from the glass all change the usable light. Check the intended position at midday before buying, and remember that a spot that works in June may not work in December.

Water the root ball, not the calendar

Regular in active growth after upper drying; less in winter Use a nursery pot or another container with drainage holes, water the root ball evenly, and empty the cachepot afterwards. Pot size, root mass, light and room temperature determine the interval; the day of the week does not.

Rich, structured and free-draining mix Repot when roots and mix indicate a need rather than immediately after purchase. A much larger pot stays wet longer and can turn a simple watering error into root damage.

What changes in Dutch winter?

Move it to maximum winter light and wipe broad leaves clean. Reduce watering as growth slows, but do not let an actively lit plant remain dry for extreme periods.

During long grey stretches, clean dusty leaves, use the brightest suitable position and pause routine feeding when the plant is not growing. Keep foliage clear of both hot radiator air and cold glass. Make one change at a time so you can see what helped.

Read the plant’s signals

What you seeWhat to check first
Splitting leavesOften normal movement damage; check draughts
Curling edgesDrying, heat or root stress
Weak leaning growthInsufficient or one-sided light

One imperfect old leaf rarely requires an emergency response. Look for a pattern across new growth, soil moisture and roots, and compare with a dated photo before changing several variables.

How to propagate it

Divide a mature multi-growth plant only when distinct rooted sections exist. Seed propagation is slow.

Use clean tools, label the cutting with its date and keep new propagation in stable bright indirect light while roots establish. Share healthy, pest-free material through a local stekjesbieb or plant swap.

Safety at home

Leaves and seeds can cause gastrointestinal upset if eaten. Keep away from pets and children who chew plants.
