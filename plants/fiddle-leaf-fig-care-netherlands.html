Fiddle-leaf fig care in the Netherlands.

A practical profile for Amsterdam apartments and Dutch homes, from summer window light to winter heating.

Short answer

Place Ficus lyrata directly beside a bright window, water thoroughly after the upper mix partly dries, and keep light and temperature stable. Clean its large leaves and avoid oversized pots.

Care at a glance

Botanical nameFicus lyrata

WaterWater after partial drying; soak evenly and drain
SoilStructured, well-aerated mix in a sturdy pot
TemperatureWarm, stable and draught-free

Does it fit an Amsterdam home?

This plant needs more light than many interior photographs imply. Tall Dutch windows help, but place the canopy beside the glass rather than across the room.

Window direction is only the beginning. Opposite buildings, balconies, trees, privacy film and distance from the glass all change the usable light. Check the intended position at midday before buying, and remember that a spot that works in June may not work in December.

Water the root ball, not the calendar

Water after partial drying; soak evenly and drain Use a nursery pot or another container with drainage holes, water the root ball evenly, and empty the cachepot afterwards. Pot size, root mass, light and room temperature determine the interval; the day of the week does not.

Structured, well-aerated mix in a sturdy pot Repot when roots and mix indicate a need rather than immediately after purchase. A much larger pot stays wet longer and can turn a simple watering error into root damage.

What changes in Dutch winter?

Autumn light loss, moving the plant and turning on heating at once can trigger leaf drop. Change conditions gradually and keep roots from staying wet.

During long grey stretches, clean dusty leaves, use the brightest suitable position and pause routine feeding when the plant is not growing. Keep foliage clear of both hot radiator air and cold glass. Make one change at a time so you can see what helped.

Read the plant’s signals

What you seeWhat to check first
Dark spreading spotsRoot stress, cold or disease
Crisp tan edgesDrying, heat or damaged roots
Lower leaf dropChange in light or moisture

One imperfect old leaf rarely requires an emergency response. Look for a pattern across new growth, soil moisture and roots, and compare with a dated photo before changing several variables.

How to propagate it

Stem cuttings can root, but air layering is often more reliable on an established woody stem. A detached leaf alone will not form a tree.

Use clean tools, label the cutting with its date and keep new propagation in stable bright indirect light while roots establish. Share healthy, pest-free material through a local stekjesbieb or plant swap.

Safety at home

Ficus sap can irritate skin and the plant is unsuitable for chewing pets.
