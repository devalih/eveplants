Wax plant care in the Netherlands.

A practical profile for Amsterdam apartments and Dutch homes, from summer window light to winter heating.

Short answer

Give Hoya carnosa bright indirect light with gentle sun, a small pot of loose fast-draining mix, and time to dry partly between waterings. Do not remove old flower spurs, because the plant can bloom from them again.

Care at a glance

Botanical nameHoya carnosa
LightBright indirect light with gentle acclimated sun
WaterAllow partial drying, then water thoroughly and drain
SoilLoose epiphytic-style mix in a modest pot
TemperatureWarm with no cold draughts
FeedingLight feeding during active vine and bud growth

Does it fit an Amsterdam home?

Hoya suits a bright shelf or hanging position, but its vines may eventually need a trellis. Place it where flowers and sticky nectar will not damage delicate surfaces.

Window direction is only the beginning. Opposite buildings, balconies, trees, privacy film and distance from the glass all change the usable light. Check the intended position at midday before buying, and remember that a spot that works in June may not work in December.

Water the root ball, not the calendar

Allow partial drying, then water thoroughly and drain Use a nursery pot or another container with drainage holes, water the root ball evenly, and empty the cachepot afterwards. Pot size, root mass, light and room temperature determine the interval; the day of the week does not.

Loose epiphytic-style mix in a modest pot Repot when roots and mix indicate a need rather than immediately after purchase. A much larger pot stays wet longer and can turn a simple watering error into root damage.

What changes in Dutch winter?

Keep it close to winter light and avoid cold water or cold glass. Slower drying means a longer interval, not a permanently dry root ball.

During long grey stretches, clean dusty leaves, use the brightest suitable position and pause routine feeding when the plant is not growing. Keep foliage clear of both hot radiator air and cold glass. Make one change at a time so you can see what helped.

Read the plant’s signals

What you seeWhat to check first
Leaf dropWatering extremes, cold or relocation
No flowersLight, maturity or repeated pruning of spurs
Soft leavesRoot or moisture stress

One imperfect old leaf rarely requires an emergency response. Look for a pattern across new growth, soil moisture and roots, and compare with a dated photo before changing several variables.

How to propagate it

Take stem cuttings with nodes and root them in water, perlite or a light mix under warm bright conditions.

Use clean tools, label the cutting with its date and keep new propagation in stable bright indirect light while roots establish. Share healthy, pest-free material through a local stekjesbieb or plant swap.

Safety at home

Hoyas are often chosen for pet-aware homes, but verify the exact species and your animal’s risk with a veterinarian.
