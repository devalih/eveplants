Money tree care in the Netherlands.

A practical profile for Amsterdam apartments and Dutch homes, from summer window light to winter heating.

Short answer

Place a money tree in bright indirect light, water thoroughly once the upper part of the mix has dried, and always let excess water drain away. Keep its braided trunk warm and away from cold glass, and rotate it occasionally for even growth.

Care at a glance

Botanical namePachira aquatica
LightBright indirect light; gentle morning sun can work after acclimation
WaterWater thoroughly after the upper third of the mix dries, then empty excess water from the outer pot
SoilAiry, well-draining houseplant mix in a pot with drainage holes
TemperatureWarm, stable indoor temperatures; protect from cold draughts and cold window glass
FeedingLight feeding during active spring and summer growth

Does it fit an Amsterdam home?

A money tree makes a good floor plant for a bright Amsterdam living room, especially near an east- or west-facing window with a little distance from the glass. Its braided trunk gives it height without taking much floor space, but it will become sparse in a dark corner.

Window direction is only the beginning. Opposite buildings, balconies, trees, privacy film and distance from the glass all change the usable light. Check the intended position at midday before buying, and remember that a spot that works in June may not work in December.

Water the root ball, not the calendar

Water thoroughly after the upper third of the mix dries, then empty excess water from the outer pot Use a nursery pot or another container with drainage holes, water the root ball evenly, and empty the cachepot afterwards. Pot size, root mass, light and room temperature determine the interval; the day of the week does not.

Airy, well-draining houseplant mix in a pot with drainage holes Repot when roots and mix indicate a need rather than immediately after purchase. A much larger pot stays wet longer and can turn a simple watering error into root damage.

What changes in Dutch winter?

Dutch winter means slower drying and lower light. Move the plant closer to useful daylight, reduce watering rather than stopping it completely, and keep the roots warm by avoiding a cold floor or windowsill.

During long grey stretches, clean dusty leaves, use the brightest suitable position and pause routine feeding when the plant is not growing. Keep foliage clear of both hot radiator air and cold glass. Make one change at a time so you can see what helped.

Read the plant’s signals

What you seeWhat to check first
Yellowing lower leaves with wet soilWatering too often, poor drainage or cold roots
Crisp leaf edges or drooping with dry mixExtended dryness, heat from a radiator or low humidity
Long, sparse growthInsufficient light
Leaf drop after a moveA rapid change in light, temperature or watering pattern

One imperfect old leaf rarely requires an emergency response. Look for a pattern across new growth, soil moisture and roots, and compare with a dated photo before changing several variables.

How to propagate it

Take healthy stem cuttings with several nodes during active growth and root them in a warm, lightly moist medium. The braided form sold in shops is a group of young stems, so do not try to unbraid an established trunk.

Use clean tools, label the cutting with its date and keep new propagation in stable bright indirect light while roots establish. Share healthy, pest-free material through a local stekjesbieb or plant swap.

Safety at home

Pachira aquatica is commonly listed as non-toxic to cats and dogs, but discourage chewing and confirm suitability with a veterinarian for your own animals.
