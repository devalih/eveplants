Plant guide · Monstera deliciosa

Monstera care in the Netherlands.

A practical profile for Amsterdam apartments and Dutch homes, from summer window light to winter heating.

Short answer

Place Monstera deliciosa in bright indirect light, water thoroughly after the upper potting mix has partly dried, use a draining chunky mix and give mature stems a support. In Dutch winter, move it closer to light and expect slower drying.

Care at a glance

Botanical nameMonstera deliciosa
LightBright filtered light; gentle direct sun may be useful after gradual acclimation
WaterWater thoroughly after the upper mix partly dries, then drain fully
SoilAiry, moisture-retentive but free-draining mix in a pot with holes
TemperatureWarm and stable; protect leaves and roots from cold glass
FeedingModerate feeding during active growth; reduce when growth slows

Does it fit an Amsterdam home?

Large leaves need useful daylight and room to climb. An east, south or west window with softened intensity usually works better than a decorative position deep inside an Amsterdam room.

Window direction is only the beginning. Opposite buildings, balconies, trees, privacy film and distance from the glass all change the usable light. Check the intended position at midday before buying, and remember that a spot that works in June may not work in December.

Water the root ball, not the calendar

Water thoroughly after the upper mix partly dries, then drain fully Use a nursery pot or another container with drainage holes, water the root ball evenly, and empty the cachepot afterwards. Pot size, root mass, light and room temperature determine the interval; the day of the week does not.

Airy, moisture-retentive but free-draining mix in a pot with holes Repot when roots and mix indicate a need rather than immediately after purchase. A much larger pot stays wet longer and can turn a simple watering error into root damage.

What changes in Dutch winter?

In grey Dutch winter, move the plant closer to light and let the mix dry more slowly. Keep leaves away from cold glass, draughts and hot radiator air.

During long grey stretches, clean dusty leaves, use the brightest suitable position and pause routine feeding when the plant is not growing. Keep foliage clear of both hot radiator air and cold glass. Make one change at a time so you can see what helped.

Read the plant’s signals

What you seeWhat to check first
Yellow leaves with wet mixDrainage, low light, pot size and root condition
Brown dry edgesUneven watering, root stress, radiator air or accumulated salts
No leaf splitsPlant age, light and climbing support
Long small growthInsufficient light

One imperfect old leaf rarely requires an emergency response. Look for a pattern across new growth, soil moisture and roots, and compare with a dated photo before changing several variables.

How to propagate it

Take a stem cutting with at least one node and aerial-root nub. Root it in water or a light medium in warm, bright indirect light.

Use clean tools, label the cutting with its date and keep new propagation in stable bright indirect light while roots establish. Share healthy, pest-free material through a local stekjesbieb or plant swap.

Safety at home

Monstera contains calcium oxalate and can irritate if chewed. Keep it away from children and pets.
